## Spoken script (emotion cues are NOT spoken)

`(fast, intrigued)` In 1967, Australia's Prime Minister went for a swim… and was never seen again.

`(storytelling)` Harold Holt loved the ocean. On the seventeenth of December, he waded into rough surf at Cheviot Beach, near Portsea in Victoria.

`(tense)` The only other swimmer felt the undertow and stayed close to shore. Holt kept swimming out.

`(quiet)` Then he slipped under the waves… and was gone.

`(building)` Divers, helicopters, police, soldiers and sailors combed the coast. One of the biggest searches in Australian history.

`(serious)` His body was never found.

`(sceptical)` Rumours ran wild. One book even claimed a Chinese submarine picked him up.

`(firm)` In 2005, a coroner ruled he simply drowned.

`(amused)` His name even became slang. To "do a Harold Holt" means to bolt.

`(twist, wry)` And in 1969, a Melbourne council opened a brand-new pool: the Harold Holt Memorial Swimming Centre.

`(loop, sly)` A swimming pool… named after the man who…

## Fact notes (description / pinned comment, NOT spoken)

- Holt, Australia's 17th PM, disappeared while swimming at Cheviot Beach near Portsea, Victoria, on 17 December 1967; his body was never recovered. — https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/harold-holts-disappearance ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Harold_Holt
- Keen spearfisher/skin diver who knew the beach well; went in despite heavy swell. Alan Stewart was the only other swimmer, stayed close to shore and felt a strong undertow; Holt swam into deeper water. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Harold_Holt (citing Frame, *The Life and Death of Harold Holt*, 2005, pp. 249–250); https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-prime-minister-who-disappeared-15319213/
- Gillespie: "like a leaf being taken out… so quick and final". Search involved helicopters, police and naval divers, army; 190+ personnel on day one, later 340+; described as "one of the largest search operations in Australian history"; officially called off 5 January 1968. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-11-01/disappearance-harold-holt-inside-search-operation-australia/12817236
- 1983: British journalist Anthony Grey's book *The Prime Minister Was a Spy* claimed Holt was a Chinese spy collected by a submarine; widely rejected. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Harold_Holt
- Inquest only possible after the Coroners Act 1985 allowed inquests without a body. September 2005: Victorian State Coroner Graeme Johnstone found Holt "took an unnecessary risk and drowned in rough water"; called the conspiracy theories "fanciful". — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2005-09-02/coroner-rules-holt-conspiracy-theories-fanciful/2094682 ; https://www.smh.com.au/national/harold-holt-drowned-coroner-finds-20050902-gdlzs9.html
- "To do a Harold Holt" = rhyming slang for "bolt" (to make a rapid departure), recorded from the 1980s. — https://history.cass.anu.edu.au/centres/andc/australian-words-h
- Malvern City Council's new complex (designed from 1966, originally to be the "City of Malvern Olympic Swimming Centre") was renamed the Harold Holt Memorial Swimming Centre after his death; Holt was the local federal member. Opened by PM John Gorton on 16 March 1969. — https://vhd.heritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/places/12051 ; https://web.archive.org/web/20170608010512/www.stonnington.vic.gov.au/Discover/Harold-Holt-Swim-Centre/History-of-Harold-Holt-Swim-Centre ; https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/release/transcript-2021
- Bonus (not spoken): memorial service at St Paul's Cathedral, Melbourne, 22 Dec 1967, attended by US President Lyndon Johnson, Prince Charles and UK PM Harold Wilson; NAA calls it the largest gathering of world leaders in Australia's history. — https://www.naa.gov.au/explore-collection/australias-prime-ministers/harold-holt/after-office ; https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/98233-memorial-service-missing-prime-minister
