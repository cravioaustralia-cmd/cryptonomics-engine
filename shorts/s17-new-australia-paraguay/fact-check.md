## Claim checklist

### 1. “Hundreds of Australians once sailed to South America… to build a new Australia.”
**PASS (soft).**  
First sailing of *Royal Tar* carried on the order of **~220–238** colonists (often cited ~220 / QSA “238 passengers”). Later sailings / total movement often summarised as hundreds–~500 across waves. Soft: “Hundreds” is fair colour for the venture overall; do not invent a precise census on screen. “New Australia” is the colony name (also Nueva Australia).

### 2. “And one of them ended up on Australia’s money.”
**PASS.**  
Poet **Mary Gilmore** (née Cameron) was a New Australia / Cosme colonist and appears on the Reserve Bank of Australia **$10** note (with A. B. “Banjo” Paterson). Soft: she left Paraguay and returned to Australia; “ended up on” / “who’s now on” is fair for the note design, not a claim she stayed forever.

### 3. “1893. Australia is in recession. Striking shearers feel beaten.”
**PASS (soft).**  
1890s depression / banking crisis context; 1891 shearers’ strike (and aftermath) shaped Lane’s recruiting among unionists/shearers. Soft: “feel beaten” is colour — avoid inventing a single battle as sole cause. Soft: recession is Australia-wide mood, not only Queensland.

### 4. “Journalist William Lane promises a socialist paradise… in the jungles of Paraguay.”
**PASS.**  
Lane was a prominent journalist / labour propagandist who led the New Australia Co-operative Colonisation Society and sold a utopian socialist settlement in Paraguay. Soft: “jungles” = subtropical forest / interior settlement language used in popular accounts — OK as VO colour; prefer not to invent Amazon rainforest imagery (wrong biome branding). Correct country: **Paraguay**.

### 5. “Paraguay wants settlers after a war that wiped out many of its men.”
**PASS (soft).**  
War of the Triple Alliance (**1864–70**) devastated Paraguay’s population, especially adult males — commonly cited in New Australia narratives as why Paraguay offered large land grants to settlers. Soft: do **not** invent exact casualty percentages on screen (e.g. avoid hard “90% of men” unless sourced and needed — out of VO). VO “wiped out many of its men” is the correct soft stance.

### 6. “July 1893. The ship Royal Tar leaves Sydney with over 200 colonists.”
**PASS.**  
*Royal Tar* departed Sydney (**Mort Bay / Sydney**) **1 July 1893** with **over 200** colonists (~220–238 commonly cited). Soft: “over 200” matches VO and soft-passes vs ~220 exact. Correct departure: **Sydney, Australia** — not Melbourne/Brisbane as stand-in.

### 7. “But Lane bans alcohol, and bans mixing with Paraguayans. Only British settlers allowed.”
**PASS (soft).**  
Lane enforced **temperance** (alcohol ban), **racial exclusivity** / British-stock preference, and restricted mixing with Paraguayans. Soft: these rules were major friction points — **avoid overstating as the sole cause of failure** (hardship, governance, personality conflict, and split also matter). Soft: “British settlers” ≈ British-descended / English-speaking white colonists as Lane framed it — do not invent a modern legal immigration statute card.

### 8. “It splits within a year. Yet about 2,000 descendants still live in Paraguay.”
**PASS (soft) on split; SOFT on 2,000.**  
**Split:** Lane and followers left New Australia (May **1894**, within about a year) to found **Cosme**; New Australia continued separately — “splits within a year” PASS soft.  
**Descendants:** “about 2,000” is widely repeated in journalism / secondary summaries (QSA blog; Wikipedia *Australian Paraguayans* with citation caveats). Prefer on-screen soft wording **“about 2,000”** / **“still live”** — do **not** treat as a hard census figure. **VERIFY** if flashing an exact number without “about”. Soft: descendants concentrate around Nueva Australia / Nueva Londres and Cosme lineages; not all Paraguay.

### 9. “One colonist was poet Mary Gilmore… who’s now on Australia’s $10 note.”
**PASS.**  
Gilmore joined the Paraguay venture (New Australia / Cosme; taught there) and later became Dame Mary Gilmore; her portrait appears on the RBA **$10** (with Banjo Paterson). Soft: she returned to Australia; “who’s now on” is fair for the circulating note design. Soft: paper-series $10 (pre-polymer) is a **different** design — do not use old paper $10 stills as if they show Gilmore.

### 10. Incomplete loop — “Because”
**PASS (editorial).**  
Incomplete on purpose; cycles to the open. Not a factual claim by itself.
