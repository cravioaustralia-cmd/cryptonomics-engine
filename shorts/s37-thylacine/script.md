## Fact notes (description / pinned comment, NOT spoken)

- Tasmanian government protection came into force on 10 July 1936, 59 days before the last known thylacine died in captivity. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine ; https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/extinction-of-thylacine ("granted protected status just 59 days before the death of the last known thylacine")
- Thylacine = carnivorous marsupial known as the Tasmanian tiger or Tasmanian wolf; dark stripes across its back; marsupial pouch (both sexes had one). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine
- Bounties: farm-owner bounties from 1830; Tasmanian Government bounty from 1888 to 1909 of £1 per full-grown animal and 10 shillings per juvenile; more than 2,180 bounties awarded (Wikipedia says more than 2,200). Thylacines were blamed for sheep attacks. — https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/extinction-of-thylacine ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine
- The last known thylacine killed in the wild was shot in 1930 by Wilf Batty at Mawbanna, NW Tasmania. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine ; https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/extinction-of-thylacine ("The last known shooting of a wild thylacine took place in 1930")
- The last known thylacine died at Beaumaris Zoo (Hobart Zoo) on the night of 7 September 1936. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-12-05/last-tasmanian-tiger-remains-found-in-museum-cupboard/101733008 ; https://doi.org/10.7882/az.2023.017
- No conclusive evidence of survival since; declared extinct by the IUCN in 1982 and the Tasmanian government in 1986. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine ; NMA (above)
- National Threatened Species Day has been held on 7 September since 1996 to mark the date. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine ; ABC (above)
- December 2022: Robert Paddle and TMAG curator Kathryn Medlock identified the endling's skin and skeleton in the Tasmanian Museum and Art Gallery; the skin had been kept in a cupboard in the museum's education office and taken around the country as a travelling educational exhibit, never properly catalogued. The animal was an aged female, not a male called "Benjamin". — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-12-05/last-tasmanian-tiger-remains-found-in-museum-cupboard/101733008 ; https://www.bbc.com/news/world-australia-63855426 ; https://doi.org/10.7882/az.2023.017 (Australian Zoologist, 2023)
- Bonus (not spoken): "Benjamin" was invented in 1968 by Frank Darby, who never worked at the zoo. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thylacine (citing ABC, 5 Dec 2022, "Stop calling the last thylacine Benjamin")
- Not used, because it's disputed: the popular claim that she died after being locked out of her shelter in freezing weather. NMA says "possibly from exposure and neglect"; other researchers favour old age. Keep it out of the VO.
