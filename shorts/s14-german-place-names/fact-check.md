## Claim checklist

### 1. ~69 German place names wiped / renamed in SA during WWI
**PASS (soft-count).**  
Parliamentary nomenclature work identified **67** “enemy origin” names (1916 Select Committee / Nomenclature Committee). The **Nomenclature Act 1917** authorised alteration; **69** changes were **gazetted 10 January 1918**. Secondary sources often say “69” (AWM, germanaustralia.com, Wikipedia table).  
**VO soft wording:** Spoken “69” is fine (matches gazette). Prefer **~69** / “dozens” on-screen if hedging. Avoid “exactly 67 only” or “every German name in Australia”.

### 2. Nomenclature Act 1917 / renames often cited 1918
**PASS.**  
Act **1917**; proclamation / gazette of new names **10 January 1918**. Script’s “In 1918, the names are replaced” is correct for when the replacements took effect. Soft: do not say the Act itself was passed in 1918.

### 3. German settlers from 1838, escaping religious persecution; towns Hahndorf, Lobethal, Klemzig
**PASS (soft).**  
Lutheran / Prussian (often Silesian) refugees fleeing religious persecution arrived from the late 1830s. **Klemzig** (Augustus Kavel party) and **Hahndorf** (families from the *Zebra*, Dec 1838, plus some from Klemzig) are standard early settlements; **Lobethal** followed soon after in the Adelaide Hills. “From 1838” is fair colour.  
**Avoid:** claiming all three were founded on the same ship/day.

### 4. Hahndorf → Ambleside; Klemzig → Gaza; Blumberg → Birdwood (Gallipoli general)
**PASS.**  
All three appear on the 10 Jan 1918 gazette list. **Birdwood** commemorates **William Riddell Birdwood**, ANZAC commander at Gallipoli.  
**SOFT — Ambleside origin:** Ambleside was taken from a **nearby railway station** name (Hahndorf heritage factsheet), not from a wartime general. Do not call Ambleside a “Gallipoli general” rename.  
**SOFT — Klemzig later:** Klemzig **was restored in 1935** (with Hahndorf and Lobethal). Script correctly does **not** say Klemzig never came back — only that Hahndorf returned in 1935 and **many others never did** (e.g. Birdwood, Verdun, Peterborough).

### 5. Hahndorf named after Danish-born Capt. Hahn
**PASS.**  
Town named for **Captain Dirk Meinerts (Dirk Meinhertz) Hahn**, Danish-born master of the *Zebra*, who helped negotiate land for the Lutheran settlers. Naming after a Dane (not a German) is the intended irony — safe.

### 6. Adelaide named after German-born Queen Adelaide (Saxe-Meiningen)
**PASS.**  
Capital named for **Queen Adelaide** (Adelheid of **Saxe-Meiningen**, b. Meiningen 1792), consort of William IV. German-born by origin; later Queen of the United Kingdom / Hanover.  
**VO soft wording:** “German-born queen” is accurate. Soft: she was British queen consort — do not imply Adelaide (the city) was a German colony or that the name was “enemy origin” under the Act (it was never renamed).

### 7. Hahndorf restored 1935; many names never restored (Birdwood etc.)
**PASS.**  
**Nomenclature Act 1935** (assented 12 Dec 1935) restored **Hahndorf**, **Lobethal** (from Tweedvale), and **Klemzig** (from Gaza). **Birdwood** (ex-Blumberg), **Verdun** (ex-Grunthal), **Peterborough** (ex-Petersburg), and many others **kept** wartime names. Further partial restorations occurred in the 1970s–80s (~20), but “many others never did” remains true.  
**Soft for Claude:** If VO mentions Lobethal as a settler town, do not imply Lobethal stayed Ambleside-style forever — it also returned in 1935. Birdwood is the clean “never restored” example for the Blumberg beat.
