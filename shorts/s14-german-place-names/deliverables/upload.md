## Description
During World War One, South Australia wiped 69 German place names off the map, and missed the most obvious one. German settlers had arrived from 1838, escaping religious persecution, and built towns like Hahndorf, Lobethal and Klemzig. In 1918 the names were replaced: Hahndorf became Ambleside, Klemzig became Gaza, and Blumberg became Birdwood, after a Gallipoli general. But Hahndorf was named after a Danish-born sea captain. And the capital, Adelaide, is named after a German-born queen. Hahndorf got its name back in 1935. Many others never did.
