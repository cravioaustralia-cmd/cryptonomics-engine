## Claim checklist

### 1. “Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight birds… and lost.”
**PASS (soft framing).**  
1932 military cull of emus in WA used **Lewis machine guns**; operation widely judged ineffective / abandoned amid ridicule — popular “lost” framing is fair VO colour. Soft: it was a nuisance wildlife cull, not a declared war against a state; do not invent battle medals or army-wide mobilisation on screen.

### 2. “1932. About 20,000 emus are wrecking wheat farms in Western Australia.”
**PASS / PASS-SOFT on 20,000.**  
Year **1932** and **WA wheatbelt** farms (Campion / Chandler–Walgoolan area) are solid. “About **20,000** emus” is the standard secondary figure (Britannica; Wikipedia citing period context). Soft: keep **“about”** — not a hard census. Soft: “wrecking” = crop damage / fence damage colour — OK.

### 3. “So the army sends soldiers with machine guns.”
**PASS.**  
Defence Minister **Sir George Pearce** authorised Royal Australian Artillery personnel under **Major G.P.W. Meredith** (with Sergeant S. McMurray and Gunner J. O’Halloran), armed with **two Lewis guns** and ~**10,000** rounds. Soft: force was small (three gunners), not a large infantry column — VO “soldiers” is still accurate.

### 4. “Problem one: the emus split up and scatter.”
**PASS.**  
Contemporary and scholarly accounts emphasise flocks splitting into small, mobile groups (“guerrilla tactics” in Serventy’s oft-cited summary) making machine-gun fire uneconomic.

### 5. “Problem two: they run over 40 kilometres an hour.”
**PASS.**  
Emus can sprint about **48 km/h** (ABC Science citing classic observation; species references commonly ~48 km/h / ~30 mph). Soft: “over 40” is safely under the accepted sprint figure — do not invent 70+ km/h on screen.

### 6. “Problem three: many kept running even after being hit.”
**PASS-SOFT.**  
Meredith and later popular accounts emphasise striking manoeuvrability **even while badly wounded**; birds compared to tanks / hard to stop. Soft: keep colour as VO “many kept running…” — avoid graphic wound stills; no gore on screen (dignity rule). Soft: do not invent a medical study of hit rates.

### 7. “After weeks, thousands of bullets, and newspapers mocking them… the soldiers were pulled out.”
**PASS / PASS-SOFT on duration.**  
- **Weeks:** first deployment from **2 November**; first withdrawal **8 November**; second phase **13 November–10 December** when Meredith recalled — overall campaign spans **weeks** (about a month+). Soft: “after weeks” is fair; avoid hard “two months” unless needed.  
- **Thousands of bullets:** ~**9,860** rounds claimed in Meredith’s report / ~**10,000** allotted — “thousands” PASS. Soft: do not flash exact 9,860 as undisputed gospel (kill:rounds ratio disputed).  
- **Newspapers mocking:** national/international ridicule and parliamentary mockery documented (Britannica; period press; ABC).  
- **Pulled out:** soldiers/guns withdrawn (Nov 8 first pull; Dec 10 final recall) — PASS.

### 8. “The officer in charge reportedly said that with an army of birds like these, he could take on any army in the world.”
**PASS-SOFT (script already softens with “reportedly”).**  
Widely attributed to **Major G.P.W. Meredith** after the operation, commonly quoted in the form: *“If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds, it would face any army in the world…”* (often with tank/Zulu follow-ons). Cited via *Sunday Herald* (Sydney) **5 July 1953** special correspondent piece and repeated in SMH / ABC / Wikipedia / SciAm summary. Soft: keep **“reportedly”**; VO paraphrase “army of birds like these” is acceptable colour — do **not** invent a verbatim on-screen quote card unless matching a sourced line. Soft: officer = Meredith (RAA) — OK to label `MAJOR MEREDITH` if used.

### 9. Loop — “Which is how Australia sent soldiers to fight birds… and lost.”
**PASS (editorial echo).**  
Loops the open; not a new factual claim.
