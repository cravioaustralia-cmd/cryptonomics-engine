## Claim checklist

### 1. “A ship sank off Australia, and only three things survived. Two teenagers… and a peacock.”
**PASS-SOFT.**  
Only **two people** survived the wreck (Tom Pearce & Eva Carmichael). The peacock is a **cargo artefact** that washed ashore intact in its crate — popular “third survivor” framing used by museums/tourism. Soft: do **not** imply the peacock was a living animal or a third human survivor. Soft: other cargo also came ashore; peacock is the famous intact luxury piece.

### 2. “1878. The Loch Ard hits rocks before dawn. Fifty-four people on board.”
**PASS.**  
Wreck: **1 June 1878**, before dawn / early morning mist off Mutton Bird Island near Port Campbell. Standard count **54** souls (crew + passengers; sources commonly 36–37 crew + 16–18 passengers totalling 54). Soft: some contemporary accounts round passenger/crew splits differently — keep **54** as the VO figure; 52 deaths / 2 survivors.

### 3. “Tom, an 18-year-old apprentice, is washed into a gorge. Then he hears screaming. It’s Eva, also 18, who’s just lost her entire family. He swims back out and drags her to shore.”
**PASS-SOFT (ages).**  
- **Names:** Tom (Thomas Richard) Pearce, apprentice; Eva (Eva / Eveline) Carmichael, passenger — PASS.  
- **Rescue sequence:** Pearce washed into what is now **Loch Ard Gorge**, heard Carmichael, swam back out, brought her ashore — PASS (Heritage Victoria / period testimony).  
- **Family:** Eva’s parents and siblings on board drowned — PASS (“entire family” on that voyage). Soft: she later had a life in Ireland/England; “entire family” = the family travelling with her.  
- **Ages:** Script says **18**. National Portrait Gallery and several heritage summaries say **18**; other secondary sources (incl. some Wikipedia / Google Arts) say **19**. Soft: keep VO **18**; do **not** slam a hard conflicting age card; optional soft chip `~18` if needed. PASS-SOFT.

### 4. “The whole country wanted them to marry. They didn’t.”
**PASS.**  
Period press and public sentiment romanticised a marriage; they did **not** marry. Eva returned to Ireland and later married Captain Thomas Achilles Townshend (1884). Tom later married Edith Strasenburgh. Soft: “whole country” is popular hyperbole for colonial celebrity — OK for VO; avoid a literal nationwide-poll graphic.

### 5. “And the third survivor? A life-size ceramic peacock, washed ashore in its crate… almost without a scratch. Today it’s valued at millions.”
**PASS-SOFT (value).**  
- **Object:** Life-size **Minton** majolica / ceramic peacock (Paul Comolera design), destined for the **1880 Melbourne International Exhibition** — PASS.  
- **Recovery:** Washed ashore in packing crate at Loch Ard Gorge area, found largely intact (head later reattached historically) — PASS-SOFT “almost without a scratch.” Soft: do not claim perfect factory-new condition.  
- **Home:** Displayed at **Flagstaff Hill Maritime Museum / Village, Warrnambool** — PASS.  
- **“Valued at millions”:** Widely reported **insurance / heritage valuation ~A$4 million** (c.2010 listings). Soft: this is **museum insurance / marketing valuation**, **not** a recent public auction hammer for *this* piece. Other Minton peacocks have auctioned far lower (e.g. ~US$170k class comps). Soft: on-screen prefer `VALUED AT MILLIONS` / soft `$4M INSURED` — **never** invent a live market price or “sold for millions.” PASS-SOFT / VERIFY before any hard dollar slam.

### 6. Loop — “Only three things survived. Two teenagers… and a peacock.”
**PASS (editorial incomplete loop / echo).**  
Same soft notes as claim 1; no new factual claim.
