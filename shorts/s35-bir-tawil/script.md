## Spoken script (emotion cues are NOT spoken)

`(fast, intrigued)` There's a piece of land almost the size of the ACT… and no country on Earth will claim it.

`(storytelling)` It's called Bir Tawil. A patch of desert between Egypt and Sudan.

`(building)` In 1899, Britain and Egypt drew the border dead straight, along the twenty-second parallel.

`(puzzled)` Three years later, the British drew a second line, based on which tribes used the land.

`(twist)` Here's the catch. Egypt goes by the first line. That gives it the Halaib Triangle, ten times bigger, right on the Red Sea.

`(sly)` Sudan goes by the second line. That gives it Halaib too.

`(deadpan)` And each country's line hands Bir Tawil to the other. Claim it… and you give up Halaib.

`(amused)` In 2014, an American dad planted a flag there so his daughter could be a real princess.

`(serious)` No government recognised it.

`(loop, sly)` And that's why, to this day…

## Fact notes (description / pinned comment, NOT spoken)

- Bir Tawil is about 2,060 km² of land on the Egypt–Sudan border claimed by neither country; it is the only habitable place not claimed by any recognised government. No surface water, no permanent settlement, no sea access. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil (citing US State Dept International Boundary Study, 1962; Nine News, 5 Oct 2024)
- ACT is 2,358 km², so Bir Tawil (~2,060 km²) is about 87% of its size ("almost the size of the ACT"). — https://www.ga.gov.au/scientific-topics/national-location-information/dimensions/area-of-australia-states-and-territories
- 19 January 1899: UK–Egypt agreement defined Sudan as the territories south of the 22nd parallel (the straight "political boundary"). 4 November 1902: the UK drew a separate "administrative boundary" reflecting the tribes' actual use of the land (Ababda grazing land south of the line went to Egypt's administration; Halaib north of the line to Sudan's). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaib_Triangle
- Egypt asserts the 1899 line (Halaib to Egypt, Bir Tawil to Sudan); Sudan asserts the 1902 line (Halaib to Sudan, Bir Tawil to Egypt). So both claim Halaib and neither claims Bir Tawil, because claiming it would undercut their claim to Halaib. — same; also https://www.theguardian.com/world/2016/mar/03/welcome-to-the-land-that-no-country-wants-bir-tawil
- Halaib Triangle is 20,580 km² (about ten times Bir Tawil) on the Red Sea coast; Egypt has controlled it since the 1990s. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaib_Triangle
- June 2014: Virginia farmer Jeremiah Heaton reached Bir Tawil, planted a flag and proclaimed the "Kingdom of North Sudan" so his daughter Emily could be "an actual princess". — The Guardian, 3 Mar 2016 (link above)
- No claim to Bir Tawil has been recognised by any government or reputable international organisation. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil (citing The Guardian; Business Insider, 15 Nov 2017)
- Note: Ababda and Bishari people pass through the area and there are informal gold-mining camps, so "no permanent residents" rather than "nobody ever goes there". Script avoids saying no one is there.
