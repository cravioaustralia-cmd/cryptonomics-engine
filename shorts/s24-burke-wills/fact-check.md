## Claim checklist

### 1. “In 1860, two men set out to cross Australia… and missed their rescue by just nine hours.”
**PASS-SOFT.**  
Expedition departed **Royal Park, Melbourne, 20 August 1860**. Popular culture frames **Burke & Wills** as the two headline explorers; the full Victorian Exploring Expedition was larger, and the Gulf dash was **four** men (Burke, Wills, King, Gray). Soft: open “two men” is classic PASS-SOFT framing — on-screen prefer labels `BURKE & WILLS` / `1860` / `NINE HOURS` without a hard “only two departed Melbourne” stamp.  
**Nine hours:** depot party under **William Brahe** left Cooper Creek depot morning of **21 April 1861**; Burke, Wills and King arrived that **evening (~7:30 pm)** — classic ~**nine hours** miss. PASS-SOFT (widely attested; exact clock times vary slightly by source).

### 2. “Their leader, Robert O’Hara Burke, is an Irish police officer with no exploring experience.”
**PASS.**  
Burke: Irish-born; Victorian police superintendent (Castlemaine); prior military/police career; **no exploration, surveying, or navigation experience** when appointed leader (NMA; burkeandwills.net.au). Soft: he had European military service — “police officer” is fair VO shorthand for his Australian role.

### 3. “They leave Melbourne with camels, horses, about 20 tonnes of gear… and even an oak table.”
**PASS.**  
Contemporary / museum accounts: lavishly equipped party with camels and horses; stores often cited as **~20–21 tonnes**; includes a **cedar-topped oak camp table** (and chairs) among eccentric gear. Soft: “about 20 tonnes” already hedges — do not invent a precise kilogram stamp.

### 4. “But one wagon breaks down before they even leave the park. By the first night, they’ve barely reached the edge of Melbourne.”
**PASS.**  
*Age* / expedition accounts: wagon **pole broke attempting to start** at Royal Park; further wagon failures between Park and Essendon. First night camp at **Essendon / Moonee Ponds** — only a few miles from Royal Park; fair “edge of Melbourne” vernacular for 1860. Soft: some sources say ~4 miles / village of Essendon — avoid inventing a modern CBD kilometre counter unless approximated softly.

### 5. “So Burke starts dumping supplies. At Menindee he leaves most of the gear behind. At Cooper Creek, in the middle of the desert, he splits the team again.”
**PASS.**  
Burke progressively lightened / reorganised: dumped or left stores; at **Menindee (NSW)** left much of the expedition and heavy gear; at **Cooper Creek** established depot and split again for the Gulf dash. Soft: “middle of the desert” is vernacular for arid Cooper Creek country — not a literal empty sand-sea graphic only.

### 6. “He tells four men to wait there for three months, and races north with just three companions: Wills, King and Gray.”
**PASS.**  
Burke instructed depot party (Brahe in charge) to wait **three months**; northern party = Burke + **Wills, King, Gray**. Soft: exact depot roster size/names beyond Brahe not required on-screen; “four men” matches standard telling.

### 7. “After nearly two months, they reach the Gulf of Carpentaria… but thick mangrove swamps stop them from ever seeing the ocean.”
**PASS.**  
Left Cooper mid-December 1860; reached Gulf region **~9–11 February 1861** (nearly two months). Mangrove / swamp country blocked a clear walk to open ocean — they did **not** stand on an ocean beach. Soft: **never** show a tropical beach-holiday “first Europeans at the shore” hero; mangrove/coastal swamp cutaway on the map is correct.

### 8. “On the way back, food runs out. They start killing their camels for meat. Gray dies just four days from the depot.”
**PASS.**  
Return journey: starvation; camels killed for meat; **Charles Gray died 17 April 1861**; survivors reached depot **21 April** — **four days** later. Soft: cause of Gray’s death debated (dysentery / exhaustion / conflict accounts) — VO does not claim a medical cause; do not invent one on-screen.

### 9. “The men waiting at Cooper Creek hold on for over four months. Then they give up, bury food under a tree, carve a message into it, “DIG”… and leave.”
**PASS.**  
Depot party waited from mid-December 1860 to **21 April 1861** — **over four months** (past the three-month order). Brahe buried a cache and blazed trees including **DIG** (+ date / camp marks) — the **Dig Tree** folklore. Soft: exact blaze text varies (`DIG 3 FT NW` / date forms) — on-screen `DIG` is enough; do not invent a full carved paragraph unless matching a cited blaze photo.

### 10. “That same evening, Burke, Wills and King stumble into camp. They missed them by nine hours.”
**PASS / PASS-SOFT.**  
Same day **21 April 1861**, evening arrival; ~nine-hour miss (see claim 1). Soft: they found the cache / DIG message — “stumble into camp” is fair vernacular for exhausted arrival at the abandoned depot.

### 11. “Too weak to follow, they stay by the creek. Burke and Wills both die there.”
**PASS.**  
Too weak to pursue Brahe south; after a Mount Hopeless attempt they returned to Cooper Creek country; **Burke and Wills died late June 1861** along the creek. Soft: deaths were of starvation/exhaustion after weeks by the creek — “die there” = Cooper Creek area, not necessarily under the Dig Tree itself. Prefer creek / Cooper labels over a false “died at Dig Tree” stamp.

### 12. “Only King survives… because local Yandruwandha people feed and shelter him until a rescue party finds him.”
**PASS.**  
**John King** survived with care from **Yandruwandha** people until Howitt’s relief party found him (**September 1861**). Soft: depict **dignified** Indigenous assistance — no caricature, no “savage/primitive” framing, no stolen-culture props. Prefer soft chips `YANDRUWANDHA` · `KING SURVIVES` · `RESCUE 1861`.

### 13. Loop — “And it’s why, to this day, people still tell the story of how”
**PASS (editorial incomplete loop).**  
No new factual claim; bridges into open.
