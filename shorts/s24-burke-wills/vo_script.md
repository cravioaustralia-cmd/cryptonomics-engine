(fast, intense) In 1860, two men set out to cross Australia… and missed their rescue by just nine hours.

(storytelling) Their leader, Robert O’Hara Burke, is an Irish police officer with no exploring experience.

(amused) They leave Melbourne with camels, horses, about 20 tonnes of gear… and even an oak table.

(fast) But one wagon breaks down before they even leave the park. By the first night, they’ve barely reached the edge of Melbourne.

(tense) So Burke starts dumping supplies. At Menindee he leaves most of the gear behind. At Cooper Creek, in the middle of the desert, he splits the team again.

(dramatic) He tells four men to wait there for three months, and races north with just three companions: Wills, King and Gray.

(awed) After nearly two months, they reach the Gulf of Carpentaria… but thick mangrove swamps stop them from ever seeing the ocean.

(dark) On the way back, food runs out. They start killing their camels for meat. Gray dies just four days from the depot.

(tense) The men waiting at Cooper Creek hold on for over four months. Then they give up, bury food under a tree, carve a message into it, “DIG”… and leave.

(twist, stunned) That same evening, Burke, Wills and King stumble into camp. They missed them by nine hours.

(slow, eerie) Too weak to follow, they stay by the creek. Burke and Wills both die there.

(twist) Only King survives… because local Yandruwandha people feed and shelter him until a rescue party finds him.
