`(intrigued)` There's an island that changes country… every six months.

`(storytelling)` Pheasant Island. A tiny scrap of land in the Bidasoa River, wedged between France and Spain.

`(amused)` And there aren't even any pheasants on it.

`(building)` In 1659, after decades of war, the two kingdoms met here to end the fighting.

`(serious)` They signed the Treaty of the Pyrenees. Louis the Fourteenth married a Spanish princess to seal the peace.

`(deadpan)` One leftover problem: who got the island?

`(twist)` So they shared it. Still do. Spain runs it from February to July. France takes over from August to January.

`(matter-of-fact)` Nobody lives there. You're not allowed to visit. About seven thousand square metres of grass… and a stone monument.

`(loop, sly)` And in six months' time, without a single shot fired…

## Fact notes (description / pinned comment, NOT spoken)

- Pheasant Island (French Île des Faisans / Île de la Conférence; Spanish Isla de los Faisanes; Basque Konpantzia) is an uninhabited river island in the Bidasoa between Hendaye (France) and Irun (Spain). Area ~0.682 ha / ~6,820 m²; ~200 m long × ~40 m wide (eroding; figures vary slightly by source). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheasant_Island ; BBC, 28 Jan 2018 — https://www.bbc.com/news/stories-42817859
- Franco-Spanish condominium: joint sovereignty is permanent; administrative jurisdiction alternates every six months — Spain 1 February–31 July; France 1 August–31 January. Often called the world's smallest condominium; among the oldest still operating. Jurisdiction detail fixed by the Bayonne convention of 27 March 1901. — same; French MFA notice — https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/files/files/le-ministere/lieux-emblematiques/13_notice_hendaye-faisans_complete_v2.pdf
- Treaty of the Pyrenees signed on the island 7 November 1659 after ~24 conferences between Luis Méndez de Haro and Cardinal Mazarin, ending the Franco-Spanish war that had continued after the Thirty Years' War; peace sealed by the marriage of Louis XIV to Maria Theresa of Spain (daughter of Philip IV). — Wikipedia; French MFA notice
- Name is misleading: no pheasants live there; "Pheasant" is widely treated as a mistranslation / folk etymology of an older French name linked to passing/toll. Public access is forbidden except rare heritage open days; mayors of Irun and Hendaye take turns on upkeep. — Wikipedia; BBC
