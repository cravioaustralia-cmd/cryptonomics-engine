# Fact-check notes — s12-koala-fingerprints

Light check against primary / popular-science sources as of 27 Sep 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Script claims were **not** expanded beyond the supplied VO. Australian English in captions (`metres` if used; keep `crime scene` as theoretical framing).

## Claims that match published sources

| VO claim | Support |
|---|---|
| Koalas have fingerprints almost exactly like yours | Koalas (*Phascolarctos cinereus*) have dermatoglyphs (friction-ridge patterns) on digits. University of Adelaide work (Henneberg, Lambert & Leigh, 1997) described koala fingertip ridges as microscopically and macroscopically similar to human prints. Popular science (New Scientist 1996; PBS NOVA; McGill OSS) repeats “almost identical.” |
| Not similar. Almost identical. The same loops, whorls and ridges | Henneberg’s team reported the same pattern classes used in human fingerprint classification — loops, whorls and arches — formed by parallel ridges. VO’s “loops, whorls and ridges” is fair popular language (arches are the third classic class; not required on-screen). |
| Under a microscope… incredibly hard to tell apart from human prints | SEM / microscopic comparison in the Adelaide study and contemporary press: koala prints were hard to distinguish from human ones — Henneberg even noted they looked *more* human-like than chimpanzee prints in their sample. Soft at absolute “impossible,” but “incredibly hard” matches the published framing. |
| In theory, a koala could leave a print at a crime scene… that looks like a person’s | **Soft / theoretical.** Henneberg told New Scientist (1996) police should be *aware* of the possibility, while stressing it is **extremely unlikely** a koala print would appear at a crime scene. No verified Australian forensic case of a koala print being mistaken for a human suspect print was found in this check. Treat as popular-science thought experiment, not a documented case. |
| We’re not even closely related | Marsupials and placental mammals diverged tens of millions of years ago (often cited ~70–100+ Ma). Koalas are not primates. Correct popular framing. |
| Evolved fingerprints completely separately (convergent evolution) | Classic **convergent evolution / homoplasy** example: similar dermatoglyphs in lineages that have been separate since before the rise of primates. Henneberg et al. 1997 title/abstract: “Fingerprint homoplasy: Koalas and humans.” |
| Probably for gripping branches and leaves | Leading functional hypothesis in Henneberg et al.: adaptive for climbing/grasping — koalas climb eucalypts, reach, grasp leaf bunches. Fingerprints also aid tactile discrimination (leaf age/texture). “Probably” in VO correctly hedges — not a proven single-cause story. Wombats/kangaroos (related marsupials without the same arboreal leaf-picking niche) lack comparable fingertip dermatoglyphs, which supports a recent adaptive story for koalas. |
| Different animals. Different evolution. Same fingerprints | Fair punchline of convergent evolution. |

Primary refs (not exhaustive):
- Henneberg, M., Lambert, K.M., & Leigh, C.M. (1997). *Fingerprint homoplasy: Koalas and humans.* Natural Science / Adelaide Research repository summaries of the dermatoglyph study.
- New Scientist (7 Dec 1996), Ian Anderson — “You may be sweet and furry, but you’re nicked” (Henneberg quotes; crime-scene *possibility* + “extremely unlikely”).
- PBS NOVA — “Koalas have fingerprints almost identical to ours.”
- McGill University Office for Science and Society — “Koalas have fingerprints just like humans.”

## Flags / soft facts (do not invent into VO; caption restraint for Claude)

1. **“Almost identical” / “hard to tell apart”:** Supported by the Adelaide comparison and press. Do **not** stamp `INDISTINGUISHABLE` / `100% MATCH` as a forensic certainty — keep `ALMOST IDENTICAL` / `HARD TO TELL APART`.
2. **Crime-scene beat is theoretical:** No verified “koala framed for burglary” case found. Henneberg’s own hedge: extremely unlikely. MG may use a playful evidence-card / print-card motif; **do not** invent a real case file, court stamp, or news headline claiming it happened.
3. **Loops / whorls / ridges:** Solid. Optional third class = arches. No need to teach full Galton classification on-screen.
4. **Microscope:** Study used scanning electron microscopy among methods. Lab optical-microscope still in `images/` is a **generic lab proxy**, not a photo of Henneberg’s koala SEM. Label `MICROSCOPE` / `STUDY` — do not caption as “Adelaide 1997 lab photo.”
5. **Convergent evolution timing:** Popular figures vary (~70 Ma / ~80 Ma / >100 Ma). Avoid a hard `80 MILLION YEARS` stamp unless Claude wants a soft `~70–100 Ma` chip; VO does not require a number.
6. **Grip / leaves hypothesis:** Best available functional story; still a hypothesis. Keep `GRIP` / `BRANCHES` / `LEAVES` labels; avoid `PROVEN PURPOSE` language.
7. **Only non-primate with true fingerprints?** Often claimed in popular science (primates otherwise). Soft at the edges of comparative mammalogy — do not stamp `ONLY ANIMAL ON EARTH` without hedge. VO does not make that claim; don’t add it in MG.
8. **Paw still note:** Best free-licence close-up located is a **hind-foot underside** (`s12_03_…`, Clancy / Echidna Walkabout) showing ridged digital pads. True fingertip dermatoglyphs are on the **forepaw** digits; free-licence front-pad micrographs were not available. Pair paw still with **human fingerprint stills** + optional **SVG-drawn ridge overlays** for the “print” graphic. Do not claim the hind-foot photo *is* a forensic fingerprint card.
9. **Species:** Stills must be *Phascolarctos cinereus*. Australian habitat preferred (Cape Otway, Mount Lofty, Sydney, climbing/eucalyptus).
10. **No AI stills** of fake crime scenes, fake SEM koala prints, or invented Adelaide lab photos.
