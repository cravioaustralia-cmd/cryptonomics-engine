## Spoken script (emotion cues are NOT spoken)

`(fast, intrigued)` Sydney and Melbourne each refused to let the other be Australia's capital… so Australia built a whole new city.

`(amused)` The new Constitution split the difference. The capital had to be in New South Wales… but at least a hundred miles from Sydney.

`(wry)` Until it was built, Parliament would sit in Melbourne.

`(building)` Albury, Tumut and Bombala were in the running. After seven years of arguing, Parliament picked the Yass–Queanbeyan district.

`(storytelling)` Then, a worldwide design competition. One hundred and thirty-seven entries. The winner was Walter Burley Griffin, a Chicago architect, working with his wife, Marion Mahony Griffin.

`(tense)` He clashed with officials for years. By the end of 1920, he was off the job.

`(sombre)` He died in India in 1937… long before his lake was filled.

`(twist)` In 1964, there was a push to name it Lake Menzies. Robert Menzies said no, and called it Lake Burley Griffin.

`(loop, sly)` A whole capital city… all because…

*(~150 words)*

## Loop note

Last line is intentionally unresolved; hard cut back to "Sydney and Melbourne each refused to let the other be Australia's capital…", so it reads as "all because… Sydney and Melbourne each refused…".

## Fact notes (description / pinned comment, NOT spoken)

- The Sydney–Melbourne rivalry was such that neither city would agree to the other becoming the capital; the compromise made Melbourne the temporary capital while a new one was built. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Canberra (citing NAA research guide, p. 7: https://www.naa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-06/research-guide-government-records-about-ACT.pdf) ; https://www.aph.gov.au/Visit_Parliament/Art/Stories/Parliament_and_the_new_Federal_capital_1927
- Constitution s 125: the seat of government "shall be in the State of New South Wales, and be distant not less than one hundred miles from Sydney… The Parliament shall sit at Melbourne until it meet at the seat of Government." — https://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/cth/consol_act/coaca430/s125.html ; https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/how-parliament-works/the-australian-constitution/the-australian-constitution-online/chapter-vii
- Sites recommended or voted for along the way included Albury (1903 commissioners), Tumut (1903 House ballot) and Bombala (Senate amendment); it "would take seven years, seven governments and three parliaments to resolve it". The Seat of Government Act 1908, assented 14 December 1908, specified the Yass–Queanbeyan area. — https://www.foundingdocs.gov.au/item-did-110.html (National Archives of Australia, Documenting a Democracy)
- Federal Capital Design Competition launched by Minister for Home Affairs King O'Malley in May 1911; 137 entries; Griffin (entrant 29) declared winner on 23 May 1912, Eliel Saarinen second, Alfred Agache third. Griffin's entry address was Steinway Hall, Chicago. — https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra ; https://www.archives.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/562631/Report_in_Regards_to_Competitive_Designs.pdf
- The plan, submitted in Walter's name, was designed collaboratively with his wife and professional partner, architect Marion Mahony Griffin, who produced the renderings. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Burley_Griffin ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Mahony_Griffin
- Griffin was appointed Federal Capital Director of Design and Construction in October 1913; years of disputes with bureaucrats followed (a 1917 Royal Commission found officials had undermined him by supplying false data). The Hughes government removed him as director and he left the project in December 1920. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Burley_Griffin ; https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/griffin-walter-burley-443/text11115
- Griffin died of peritonitis in Lucknow, India, on 11 February 1937. — https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/griffin-walter-burley-443/text11115
- Scrivener Dam's valves were closed on 20 September 1963; the lake reached its planned level on 29 April 1964; Menzies commemorated the filling on 17 October 1964. Despite pressure within his own party for the name "Lake Menzies", he insisted on "Lake Burley Griffin" because there was no monument to Griffin. — https://www.nca.gov.au/attractions/scrivener-dam ; https://www.menziesvirtualmuseum.org.au/the-1960s/1964 (Menzies, The Measure of the Years, 1970, pp. 147–8)
- Parliament met for the last time in Melbourne on 24 March 1927; the Duke of York opened the provisional Parliament House in Canberra on 9 May 1927. — https://www.aph.gov.au/Visit_Parliament/Art/Stories/Parliament_and_the_new_Federal_capital_1927 ; https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/building-provisional-parliament-house
- Bonus (not spoken): "Burley" was Griffin's middle name (his mother's maiden name), not part of his surname; the lake's name reflects a long-running misconception. — https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/griffin-walter-burley-443/text11115 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Burley_Griffin
- Not used, because it's disputed: the popular claim that "Canberra" means "meeting place". Keep it out of the VO.
