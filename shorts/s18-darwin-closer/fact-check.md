# s18 fact-check — Darwin closer to foreign capitals / 1942 attack

**Status key:** PASS = claim safe for VO as worded (or with noted soft wording) · SOFT = true but phrase carefully · FAIL = do not use as stated · VERIFY = confirm before hard on-screen claim.

**Sources consulted (brief):** timeanddate / Geodatos great-circle distances; Wikipedia *Bombing of Darwin (February 1942)*; Library & Archives NT “The Bombing of Darwin”; Darwin Aviation Museum / secondary “more bombs” compare; Spectator Australia (Lewis & Ingman *Carrier Attack* figures); soft secondary on Mitsuo Fuchida dual command.

**Maps rule:** Darwin · Canberra · Dili · Port Moresby · Jakarta only for distance compares; Pearl Harbor labelled compare only if needed — parchment overlays on real satellite/topo; no wrong-country maps.

## Claim checklist

### 1. “One Australian city is closer to several foreign capitals than to its own.”
**PASS.**  
Darwin (NT) is commonly noted as closer to multiple foreign capitals (incl. Dili, Port Moresby, Jakarta, and others) than to Canberra. Soft: “several” is fair; do not invent an exact count of capitals on screen unless sourced.

### 2. “And that geography nearly cost it everything.”
**PASS (soft / editorial colour).**  
VO colour linking Top End proximity to the 1942 vulnerability narrative — not a literal counterfactual claim. Soft: do not invent invasion plans as hard on-screen fact beyond script.

### 3. “Darwin. Top of Australia. Canberra is over 3,100 kilometres away.”
**PASS.**  
Great-circle Darwin↔Canberra ≈ **3,127 km** (timeanddate / Geodatos). Soft “over 3,100” / “about 3,100” OK. **VERIFY** if flashing a hard exact km without ≈/about. Correct capitals: Darwin (NT) · Canberra (ACT).

### 4. “Dili, Timor-Leste? About 700 kilometres. Port Moresby? About 1,800.”
**PASS (soft).**  
Great-circle approx: Darwin↔Dili ≈ **720 km**; Darwin↔Port Moresby ≈ **1,817 km**. Soft “about 700” / “about 1,800” match VO. **FAIL** only if wildly wrong (e.g. swapping cities or claiming Dili > Canberra). Correct: Dili = Timor-Leste; Port Moresby = Papua New Guinea.

### 5. “Even Jakarta is closer than Canberra.”
**PASS.**  
Darwin↔Jakarta ≈ **2,729 km** vs Darwin↔Canberra ≈ **3,127 km** — Jakarta is closer. Soft: keep compare relative; no need for exact Jakarta km on screen unless labelled “about 2,700”.

### 6. “In 1942, that closeness becomes a danger.”
**PASS (soft / narrative).**  
Editorial bridge into the February 1942 raids. Soft: proximity was one strategic context among many — do not invent sole-cause geography determinism as hard fact.

### 7. “19 February. 188 Japanese aircraft attack Darwin.”
**PASS (soft).**  
First major Darwin air raids: **19 February 1942**. First carrier wave commonly cited as **188** aircraft (plus later land-based wave in some tallies — total force often summarised ~242 including land-based). Soft: “188” for the first/carrier strike as commonly recounted — PASS soft. Soft: do not invent exact death tolls on screen (out of VO).

### 8. “Led by the same commander who led the Pearl Harbor attack ten weeks earlier.”
**PASS (soft).**  
Commonly recounted: **Mitsuo Fuchida** led the first Pearl Harbor wave (7 Dec 1941) and commanded the Darwin carrier air attack (19 Feb 1942). Soft: “led” = air-attack leadership as commonly recounted — do **not** invent other commander names on screen without source. Soft: “ten weeks earlier” — 7 Dec 1941 → 19 Feb 1942 ≈ **10 weeks 5 days** — PASS soft.

### 9. “By many accounts, more bombs fell on Darwin than on Pearl Harbor.”
**SOFT — keep caveat.**  
Popular / museum accounts often state more **bombs** (count) fell on Darwin than Pearl Harbor; detailed histories note **tonnage / weapon weight** comparisons are nuanced (Pearl Harbor used torpedoes; Darwin bomb count often higher, tonnage debate). **Keep VO “by many accounts”** — do **not** hard-claim tonnage superiority without caveat. Soft: prefer “more bombs” (count) with soft wording over inventing kg figures on screen.

### 10. “It’s still the largest foreign attack ever made on Australia.”
**PASS (soft).**  
Fair for scale of foreign **air attack on Australian soil** / largest single-day foreign air assault on Australia as popularly framed. Soft: avoid overclaiming “only invasion”, “only attack”, or erasing later NT raids / Broome etc. Prefer “largest foreign attack” as VO — do not expand to “only time Australia was attacked”.

### 11. Incomplete loop — “Because remember:”
**PASS (editorial).**  
Incomplete on purpose; cycles to the open. Not a factual claim by itself.
