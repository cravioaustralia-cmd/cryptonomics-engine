## Claim checklist

### 1. “The world’s largest herd of wild camels isn’t in Arabia or Asia… it’s in Australia.”
**PASS.**  
Australia holds the world’s **largest feral / free-ranging camel population** (one-humped dromedaries). Guinness records “more than 1 million” (2023 framing). Soft: biologists distinguish **feral** (descended from domestic stock) from the critically endangered true wild Bactrian (*Camelus ferus*) in China/Mongolia (~900–1,000). VO “wild” = free-ranging / feral vernacular — OK for Shorts; on-screen prefer `WILD` / `FERAL` / `OVER A MILLION` without claiming IUCN “wild species” status. Soft: “herd” is collective vernacular for the national population, not one single cohesive herd.

### 2. “And the people who brought them have a famous train named after them.”
**PASS (tease; resolved by later Ghan line).**  
Cameleers and camel imports are widely linked in popular culture and railway marketing to **The Ghan**. Soft: naming history is more complex (see claim 6) — this line is a tease, not a hard etymology slam.

### 3. “1860s. Horses struggle in the outback. So Australia imports thousands of camels, mostly from what’s now India and Pakistan.”
**PASS.**  
Major camel import programmes from the **1860s** (Burke & Wills / Landells 1860; Elder / Stuckey Beltana mid-1860s onward), expanding through late 19th century. Soft: “thousands” is fair across the decades (often cited ~10,000–20,000 camels landed 1870–1900 class totals; earlier 1860s shipments were hundreds then growing). Origin ports largely **Karachi / Bombay** and British India — regions that are **today’s India and Pakistan** (plus Afghanistan routes). Soft: some camels also from Arabia / Somaliland stock lines — “mostly” hedges correctly. Do **not** invent a single census year for “thousands.”

### 4. “With them come cameleers from Afghanistan, India and Pakistan, all nicknamed ‘Afghans’.”
**PASS.**  
Cameleers were multi-ethnic (Pashtun, Baloch, Punjabi, Sindhi, and others from Afghanistan and the Indian subcontinent). Colonial Australia nicknamed them collectively **“Afghans” / “Ghans”** regardless of exact origin. Soft: modern Pakistan did not exist as a state until 1947 — script’s “Pakistan” = regions now in Pakistan (already soft via “what’s now” on the camel line; cameleer line lists contemporary country names as origin shorthand — OK for VO; avoid a hard anachronistic passport graphic).

### 5. “They haul supplies for the railways and the telegraph.”
**PASS.**  
Cameleers hauled stores, water, mail, wool, and construction materials for inland pastoral runs, mineral fields, the **Overland Telegraph** (Adelaide–Darwin), and railway survey/construction (incl. Central Australia / Trans-Australian work). Soft: also exploration parties — not required on-screen.

### 6. “The Adelaide–Darwin train, The Ghan, is widely said to be named after them.”
**PASS.**  
Script already softens with **“widely said.”** Popular / tourism / Commonwealth Railways marketing links The Ghan to Afghan cameleers. Soft: local railway history (Pichi Richi and others) notes the 1923 “Afghan Express” nickname at Quorn had a more incidental origin, later reinterpreted as cameleer homage. Do **not** slam a hard “officially named in 1860s for cameleers” card — keep soft `WIDELY SAID` / `THE GHAN` labels.

### 7. “Then trucks replace them. The camels are set free… and today there are over a million.”
**PASS-SOFT.**  
Motor vehicles and rail from the **1920s–1940s** displaced camel transport; many animals were **released, abandoned, or escaped**, founding today’s feral herds — release was **gradual / complex**, not a single nationwide dump day. Soft: on-screen avoid a literal one-day “SET FREE” stamp implying a single event.  
**“Over a million”:** widely cited (National Feral Camel Action Plan; Guinness; WA DPIRD 2024). Soft: scientific estimates vary by era and method — CSIRO ~2008 minimum ~1M; later culling / drought / survey updates sometimes cite **hundreds of thousands to >1M**. **Never invent a precise current census** on-screen. Prefer `OVER A MILLION` / soft `~1M+` — PASS-SOFT.

### 8. “Australia now exports camels to Saudi Arabia.”
**PASS / PASS-SOFT.**  
Documented **live exports** from Australia to **Saudi Arabia** began **2002** (~118–119 head from Darwin; AP/BBC/ABC). Trade also covers other Middle East / SE Asia destinations (meat, breeding stock). Soft: volumes are modest and opportunistic by later MLA reviews; “now exports” is fair present-tense vernacular for an ongoing industry niche, not a claim of huge daily Saudi shipments. Soft: do not invent 2026 headcounts or imply Australia emptied Arabia of camels. Prefer soft export chip / amused twist energy.

### 9. Loop — “So yes.”
**PASS (editorial incomplete loop).**  
No new factual claim.
