# s16 fact-check — Nullarbor +8:45 / unofficial Central Western Time

**Status key:** PASS = claim safe for VO as worded (or with noted soft wording) · SOFT = true but phrase carefully · FAIL = do not use as stated · VERIFY = confirm before hard on-screen claim.

**Sources consulted (brief):** Wikipedia *UTC+08:45* / *Time in Australia*; ABC News 2019-04-08 (“custom time zone” / phones don’t pick it up); ABC Great Southern 2024-03-30 (“Eucla time”); Shire of Dundas Annual Report 2012–13 (¾-hour change at Caiguna); Commonwealth Railways April 1935 timetable note (Wikipedia cites ≥1935 railway use of a 45-min “Central Time”); IANA tz `Australia/Eucla`; Australian Government time-zones page (does **not** list CWST as a legal zone).

**Maps rule:** Nullarbor Plain / WA–SA border / Eucla–Madura–Border Village / Eyre Highway corridor **only** — parchment overlays on real satellite/topo; no wrong-country maps.

## Claim checklist

### 1. “A strip of Australia that runs on its own time zone that no law ever created”
**PASS (soft).**  
CWST / CWT / “Eucla time” is **not** created by state or federal statute. Australian Government time-zone materials do not list it as an official zone. Local practice along a stretch of the Eyre Highway; Shire of Dundas has acknowledged dual time practice. Soft: “no law ever created” is fair colour — avoid “illegal” or “banned”. Soft: “time zone” here means a recognised informal offset (also in IANA as `Australia/Eucla`), not a legislated zone.

### 2. “The time it runs on is bizarre” / “Plus 8 hours and 45 minutes”
**PASS.**  
Offset is **UTC+8:45** (often labelled CWST / CWT / ACWST / Eucla Time). Midway between AWST (UTC+8) and ACST (UTC+9:30): +45 min from Perth, −45 min from Adelaide (standard time; DST on the SA side widens the gap — out of VO scope unless needed).

### 3. “The Nullarbor Plain. Where Western Australia meets South Australia.”
**PASS.**  
The informal zone sits on the southeastern Nullarbor / Eyre Highway corridor spanning the WA–SA border (WA roadhouses + Border Village in SA). Correct geography only.

### 4. “Perth is 8 hours ahead of world time. Adelaide is 9 and a half.”
**PASS (soft).**  
AWST = UTC+8; ACST = UTC+9:30 (standard). Soft: “world time” = UTC. Soft: Adelaide observes daylight saving (ACDT UTC+10:30) part of the year — VO uses standard offsets; do not claim Adelaide is always +9:30 year-round on screen without a seasonal caveat if you expand.

### 5. “A few roadhouses in between just… split the difference.”
**PASS (soft — VERIFY which observe).**  
Commonly cited observers (VERIFY before listing as hard fact on screen): **Cocklebiddy, Madura, Mundrabilla, Eucla** (WA) and **Border Village** (SA). Wikipedia *UTC+08:45* also lists **Caiguna**; other sources describe the zone as running from shortly **east of Caiguna** to Border Village (~340 km in ABC 2019). Soft: not every station/pastoral property uses +8:45 — e.g. Arubiddy / Arubbidy Station has been reported on a different local offset (UTC+9 / “one hour on from WA”). Soft: police / Telstra towers may run Perth time while roadhouses run Eucla time. Prefer “a few roadhouses” / “some roadhouses” as in VO — do **not** claim every Nullarbor settlement or all of WA.

### 6. “One of the only places on Earth with a quarter-hour offset.”
**PASS (soft — do not say “the only”).**  
Quarter-hour UTC offsets are rare but **not unique**. Other examples include Nepal (UTC+5:45) and Chatham Islands (UTC+12:45). Soft VO wording “one of the only” is OK; avoid “the only place on Earth” or “unique on Earth”.

### 7. “There are signs on the highway telling drivers to change their clocks.”
**PASS.**  
Road signs on the Eyre Highway advise a **45-minute** clock change (western end / near Caiguna commonly cited). Free-licence still: `s16_01_entering_cwst_sign.jpg` (Commons: *Entering Central Western Time Zone*). ABC and Wikipedia corroborate signage. Soft: wording/placement of individual signs can vary; graffiti (“Why?”) is colour, not required.

### 8. “No government ever made it official.”
**PASS.**  
Not legally defined by WA, SA, or Commonwealth statute as an official standard zone. Local custom / practice; mapped informally; IANA includes `Australia/Eucla` for technical convenience — that is **not** statutory creation. Soft: “official” ≠ “unrecognised by locals / maps / computers”.

### 9. “Nobody’s sure exactly when it began.” / origin uncertain
**PASS (soft).**  
Origin date is **uncertain** and stories differ: telegraph-station compromise folklore; railway/transcontinental practice; Wikipedia cites Commonwealth Railways April **1935** timetable evidence of a 45-minute “Central Time” watch change on the Trans-Australian Railway (Wynbring–Rawlinna) — i.e. practice **at least** that old in a related corridor, not a clean “founding year” for today’s roadhouse CWST. Soft: do **not** invent “began in 19XX” as hard fact. VO “nobody’s sure exactly when it began” is the correct soft stance.

### 10. Incomplete loop — “Yet to this day,”
**PASS (editorial).**  
Incomplete on purpose; cycles to the open. Not a factual claim by itself.
