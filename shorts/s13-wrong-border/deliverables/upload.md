## Description
Two Australian states have a border in the wrong place, and it's legal. In 1836, Britain set South Australia's eastern border as one exact line of longitude: 141°E. Surveyors marked it through the bush with trees, mounds and piles of stone. Decades later, better instruments showed the line was a few kilometres off. That left a strip of land bigger than 1,000 square kilometres on the wrong side. South Australia fought for it for decades, until London's Privy Council ruled in 1914 that the surveyed line stays. And to this day, it does.
