## Snapshot

- **Herbert John Louis “Bert” Hinkler** (8 Dec 1892 – 7 Jan 1933) — pioneer Australian aviator from **Bundaberg**, Queensland (“Australian Lone Eagle”).
- **Childhood:** watched birds (ibis folklore); built home-made gliders; 1912 Mon Repos Beach sand-dune flights (~10 m). Soft VO “small Australian town” = Bundaberg.
- **1928 first solo England→Australia:** departed **Croydon** (London area), **7 February 1928**; landed **Darwin**, **22 February 1928** — just under **15½ days**. Aircraft: **Avro Avian** G-EBOV (tiny open-cockpit light plane). Distance commonly cited ~**18,000 km** / ~12,000 miles flown.
- **Previous record:** England–Australia **~28 days** (Ross & Keith Smith, 1919, full crew) — Hinkler almost halved it (PASS-SOFT “almost halved”).
- **Nickname:** press nicknamed him **“Hustling Hinkler”** (also a Tin Pan Alley song).
- **Solo / no crew:** first person to fly the route alone; his own mechanic; no autopilot (era-correct colour).
- **1933 death:** attempting a faster England–Australia solo; departed London **7 January 1933**; crashed in the **Pratomagno** mountains near **Arezzo**, Tuscany. Body found ~**late April / 1 May 1933** (over three months). Buried **Cimitero degli Allori**, **Florence**, with **full military honours** ordered by **Benito Mussolini**.
