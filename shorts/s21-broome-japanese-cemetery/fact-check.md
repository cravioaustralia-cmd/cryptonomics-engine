## Claim checklist

### 1. “One of the biggest Japanese cemeteries outside Japan… is in a small Australian beach town.”
**PASS-SOFT.**  
Broome’s Japanese cemetery is widely described as the **largest Japanese cemetery in Australia** (Visit Broome; Tourism WA; plaque context). Soft: “**one of** the biggest … outside Japan” is a carefully hedged VO claim — diaspora cemeteries elsewhere exist; do **not** flash a hard global-rank title (`THE BIGGEST OUTSIDE JAPAN`) without stronger sourcing. Soft: Broome is a small Kimberley beach / pearling town — “beach town” colour OK (Cable Beach / Port Drive setting).

### 2. “And the story behind it is heartbreaking.”
**PASS (editorial tone).**  
High diver mortality, cyclone losses, young ages on many headstones (NMA: half of legible names died in their twenties) — sombre framing is fair. Soft: respectful memorial tone on screen; no gore.

### 3. “Broome, Western Australia. Late 1800s. The sea here is full of pearl shell.”
**PASS.**  
By summer **1888–89** Broome had become the centre of WA’s pearling industry (NMA). Pearl shell (*Pinctada maxima* mother-of-pearl) was the commercial driver; pearls were a bonus. Soft: “late 1800s” covers the boom onset — OK. Soft: do not invent a hard “1880” founding stamp unless needed.

### 4. “Divers from Japan arrive, many from villages in Wakayama.”
**PASS.**  
Japanese divers dominated Broome’s deep-diving workforce; many came from coastal **Wakayama** prefecture (incl. Taiji — Broome sister city since 1981) with fishing / abalone diving experience (NMA). Soft: “many” not “all” — Malays, Timorese and others also worked the fleets.

### 5. “They dive deep in heavy copper helmets and lead boots.”
**PASS.**  
Standard hard-hat / diving dress: copper (or bronze) helmet, canvas suit, weighted / **lead** boots, surface air pump — used as Broome beds went deeper (~20–25 fathoms / ~36–45 m) (NMA; period Broome diving-suit still `s21_14`). Soft: “copper helmets” is standard popular wording even when some helmets were bronze alloys — OK for VO.

### 6. “The bends, drowning, cyclones. Historians estimate more than one in ten Japanese divers died each year.”
**PASS (script already softens with “Historians estimate”).**  
NMA: *“In any given year more than one in 10 of the Japanese divers died.”* Broome Museum: average ~1 in 10 men in the pearl-shell industry died each year. Causes: diver’s paralysis (the bends), drowning, cyclones (plaque: 1887 / 1908 / 1935 cyclone losses; 1914 bends year). Soft: keep **“historians estimate”** / **“more than one in ten”** — do not invent a precise annual census on screen. Soft: some popular sources float higher peak rates — stick to the scripted soft wording.

### 7. “Over 900 people now rest in Broome’s Japanese cemetery.”
**PASS.**  
On-site plaque / tourism / Find a Grave secondary: **919 people** across **707 graves**; first recorded burial **1896**. Soft: “over 900” matches plaque; prefer not to slam an unverified exact 919 as gospel if disputing restorations, but 919 is the standard plaque figure — OK as soft chip `~900` / `OVER 900`. Soft: NMA notes ~1000 legible names by 1979 with many lost — “over 900” remains safe.

### 8. “The pearling boom is one reason Japan opened its first consulate in Australia, in 1896.”
**PASS-SOFT — VERIFY city before any on-screen place stamp.**  
- **Year / first consulate:** Japan’s **first official consulate in Australia opened in 1896** — confirmed by Consulate-General of Japan (Sydney) history page and Townsville heritage sources. First consul: **Tsunejiro Nakagawa**.  
- **City:** The consulate was in **Townsville, Queensland** (Kardinia, Stanton Hill / Victoria Street), **not Broome**. Closed 1908; Sydney consulate followed 1897 / upgraded later.  
- **“One reason” / pearling boom:** Soft PASS — Japanese migration and commercial interests in **northern Australia** (pearling, sugar, shipping) motivated establishing a northern consulate; Townsville was chosen as a strategic NQ port. Soft: do **not** imply the 1896 office was in Broome. Soft: Broome later had an **honorary** consul arrangement (e.g. Mayor Archie Male ~1910) — separate from the 1896 career consulate; do not conflate on screen.  
- **On-screen guidance:** Labels OK: `1896`, `FIRST JAPANESE CONSULATE`, optionally `TOWNSVILLE` if naming the city. **Never** stamp `BROOME CONSULATE 1896`.

### 9. Loop — “That’s why”
**PASS (editorial incomplete loop).**  
No new factual claim; mid-thought bridge back to the open.
