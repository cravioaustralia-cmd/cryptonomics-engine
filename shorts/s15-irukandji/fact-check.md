# s15 fact-check — Irukandji / Smaller Than Your Fingernail

**Status key:** PASS = claim safe for VO as worded (or with noted soft wording) · SOFT = true but phrase carefully · FAIL = do not use as stated.

**Sources consulted (brief):** Australian Museum *Carukia barnesi* (bell 1–2 cm; northern Aus WA→Qld); NCBI StatPearls “Irukandji Syndrome” (onset 5–120 min; impending doom; multiple species; ~2 reported deaths via ICH); Wikipedia / Fenner & Hadok MJA 2002 (fatal envenomation cases 2002 Hamilton Island + Port Douglas/GBR); Animal Diversity Web *C. barnesi*; Diving & Hyperbaric Medicine review (Carrette et al.) — multiple carybdeids; delayed systemic symptoms.

**Maps rule:** northern Australia / Great Barrier Reef / tropical north waters **only** — parchment overlays on real satellite/topo; no wrong-country maps.

---

## Claim checklist

### 1. Size — smaller than a fingernail / ~1–2.5 cm bell
**PASS (soft).**  
Australian Museum: **1–2 cm** bell diameter, tentacles up to ~1 m. ADW / other refs: typically to ~25 mm (some reports to ~35 mm). Lab / juvenile photos can show bells of only a few mm.  
**VO soft wording:** “smaller than your fingernail” is fair colour for a mature ~1–2 cm bell. Prefer **~1–2 cm** / “about fingernail-sized” on-screen if a number is needed. Avoid “microscopic” or “invisible even under a microscope”.  
**Asset note:** size diagram `03` labels a **5 mm** bell — treat as schematic / small-specimen reference, not the sole adult size for VO.

### 2. Name — “the Irukandji” / *Carukia barnesi* vs broader Irukandji syndrome
**PASS (soft).**  
*Carukia barnesi* is the classic / first-identified cause (Jack Barnes, 1960s). **Irukandji syndrome** is now attributed to **multiple** small box jellies (carybdeids), often cited as several to ~25 implicated species worldwide (*Malo*, *Alatina*, etc.).  
**VO soft wording:** Calling it “the Irukandji” for the short is fine (common name for *C. barnesi* / the syndrome’s namesake). Soft: do **not** imply only one species on Earth causes the syndrome. Captions may say “Irukandji (*Carukia barnesi* and related)” if space allows.

### 3. Range — waters of northern Australia
**PASS.**  
Northern Australian coastline: roughly Broome (WA) across NT to tropical Queensland / GBR (Cairns–Whitsundays etc.). Seasonal peaks often wet season / Nov–May in north Qld (varies by region). Rare stray reports further south.  
**Map rule:** show **northern Australia / GBR / tropical north waters only**. Do not slap a wrong-country “world Irukandji” map as the geography beat for this short (even though similar syndromes are reported elsewhere — out of scope for VO).

### 4. Delayed onset — “about half an hour later”
**PASS (soft).**  
Literature: systemic onset typically **~5–120 minutes**, often averaged ~25–40 min / ~30 min. Beach safety signage commonly cites **20–30 min**.  
**VO soft wording:** “about half an hour later” is accurate enough. Prefer **~20–60 min** if on-screen. Avoid “instantly systemic” or “always exactly 30 minutes”.

### 5. Symptoms — crushing back/chest pain, vomiting, blood pressure spikes
**PASS.**  
Classic cluster: severe backache / chest / abdominal / limb pain; nausea and vomiting; sweating; headache; **hypertension** and tachycardia; anxiety/restlessness. Severe cases: pulmonary oedema, cardiac complications.  
Safe for VO as worded.

### 6. Sense of impending doom
**PASS.**  
Widely described hallmark — profound anxiety / sense of “impending doom”; patients may become convinced they are dying. StatPearls and clinical reviews explicitly note this.  
Safe; keep eerie tone without mocking patients.

### 7. Most survive with fast hospital treatment
**PASS (soft).**  
With supportive hospital care, most patients improve within hours to ~1–2 days; fatalities are **rare**. No specific antivenom for Irukandji syndrome (unlike *Chironex*).  
**Soft:** “Most survive with fast hospital treatment” is correct. Do not claim “always harmless” or “never needs hospital”. Avoid giving first-aid protocols as medical advice in VO (vinegar vs ice guidance has changed over time — out of scope).

### 8. Deaths have been recorded (soft — do not overstate)
**PASS (soft — careful).**  
Often cited: **two** well-known Australian fatalities in **2002** (tourist cases: Hamilton Island / Whitsundays Jan 2002; outer GBR off Port Douglas Apr 2002), both linked to severe hypertension → intracranial haemorrhage (Fenner & Hadok, MJA 2002; contemporary news). Wikipedia and reviews: when treated, a single sting is almost never fatal; “two people in Australia are believed to have died…”; unknown whether other deaths were misattributed historically.  
**VO soft wording (locked):** “deaths have been recorded” — **do not** say “kills dozens,” “routinely fatal,” or invent a large death toll. Optional softer: “rare deaths have been recorded.” Prefer not naming victims in a short unless a later documentary beat requires it.
