# Fact-check notes — s11-cassowary

Light check against museum / wildlife / encyclopaedic sources as of 27 Sep 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Script claims were **not** expanded beyond the supplied VO. Australian English in captions (`metres`, `kilometres`, `faeces` / seeds language as needed).

## Claims that match published sources

| VO claim | Support |
|---|---|
| Australia has a bird that looks like a dinosaur… and can fight like one | Southern cassowary (*Casuarius casuarius*) is a large flightless ratite; casque + black plumage often compared to dinosaur silhouette in popular science. Powerful kick with dagger claw is well documented (Australian Museum; Wildlife Queensland). Comic “fight like one” is fair tone, not a taxonomic claim. |
| The cassowary. Up to about two metres tall | Adult southern cassowaries typically ~1.5–1.8 m; large females reach ~2 m. Australian Museum / Wildlife Queensland / Wikipedia summarise “up to about 2 m.” VO hedge “about” is correct. |
| Runs around 50 kilometres an hour | Widely reported top speed ~50 km/h (31 mph) through dense forest (San Diego Zoo; secondary summaries leaning on field literature). “Around 50” is fair; avoid on-screen `EXACTLY 50`. |
| Jumps. Swims. | Can jump roughly 1.5–2 m vertically; strong swimmers crossing rivers/sea (standard species accounts). |
| Dagger-like claw over ten centimetres long | Inner (medial) toe claw commonly cited at ~10–12.5 cm (up to ~120–125 mm). “Over ten centimetres” is accurate. |
| In 1926, a teenager in Queensland tried to fight one off. It kicked him, and he died | **6 April 1926**, near **Mossman**, Far North Queensland: **Phillip McClean** (often spelled McClean; age 16) and his younger brother confronted a cassowary with clubs; Phillip was kicked in the neck and died of the wound. First well-documented modern human death attributed to a cassowary. |
| One of the very few human deaths ever recorded from a bird | Formal reviews of cassowary attacks find human fatalities extremely rare (often cited as one historical death in a large attack dataset; a later US captive-bird death is sometimes listed separately). VO’s “very few” is fair — do not stamp `ONLY ONE EVER` without nuance. |
| Cassowaries are shy | Species accounts emphasise they usually retreat into dense rainforest; most serious incidents involve birds defending themselves, chicks, or food after human approach / feeding. “Shy” is the right popular framing. |
| Rainforest gardeners, spreading seeds of plants that almost nothing else can swallow | Keystone seed dispersers for large rainforest fruits (often >200–240 plant species; fruits up to ~6 cm diameter). Among few animals that can swallow and move the largest seeds over kilometre-scale distances. Gut passage can raise germination (classic *Ryparosa* example: ~92% vs ~4%). “Rainforest gardeners” is standard outreach language (Wildlife Queensland et al.). |

## Flags / soft facts (do not invent into VO; caption restraint for Claude)

1. **Height “up to about two metres”:** Typical adults are shorter (~1.5–1.8 m); ~2 m is the upper end (large females). Keep MG as `~2 m` / `UP TO ~2 m`, not a hard average stamp.
2. **Speed “around 50 km/h”:** Widely repeated figure; primary field papers are thinner than the popular number. Soft — keep `~50 km/h`, not a precision speedometer graphic claiming lab measurement.
3. **Claw “over ten centimetres”:** Solid. Literature ranges ~10–12.5 cm. No gore — show licensed foot still + dagger outline MG; never a wound recreation.
4. **1926 death — name spelling:** **Phillip McClean** is the usual citation; variants **McClean / McLean** appear in secondary write-ups. Prefer **McClean** if naming on-screen; VO does not say the name — short labels `1926` / `QUEENSLAND` / `MOSSMAN` are enough.
5. **“Tried to fight one off”:** Boys initiated contact with clubs (not an unprovoked roadside attack). VO is compressed; MG must not invent a “monster attacks child” storyboard. No graphic neck wound.
6. **“Very few” human deaths from a bird:** Soft at the edges — depends whether captive / non-cassowary bird deaths are in scope. Keep VO wording; avoid absolute `WORLD’S ONLY` stamps.
7. **Shy vs dangerous reputation:** Both true in different frames. Twist beat should read protective / ecological, not “harmless pet.”
8. **Seed-dispersal “almost nothing else can swallow”:** True for the *largest* fruits in the Wet Tropics guild; other dispersers exist for smaller seeds. VO is fair popular science.
9. **Species / geography:** Australian bird in VO = southern cassowary subspecies in FNQ rainforests (*C. c. johnsonii* often used). Stills must be *Casuarius casuarius* (Queensland / Wet Tropics preferred). Avoid northern/dwarf cassowary or clearly non-Australian zoo context unless labelled.
10. **No AI historical stills** of the 1926 incident. Period context still is a **1920–1930 Cook Highway (Cairns–Cooktown)** State Library of Queensland PD photograph — era/region proxy only, **not** a picture of the McClean property or the bird involved.

No VO rewrite required; flags are for caption / still / MG restraint.
