 Editorial stance: we do not take a side
 This documentary does not take a side in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Its question
 is narrower: how has this conflict affected Australia and Australians? It reports what happened,
 shows the evidence on screen as each claim is spoken, and gives every side's position in its
 own terms, at equal weight. It does not tell viewers what to conclude.



 How neutrality is built into every frame
 • Equal treatment. Whenever one community's experience or position is shown, the other's is shown
    at the same size, for the same time, in the same design, with the same music level. In this chapter:
    S10 (two memories of 1948) and S12 (two population figures).
 • One neutral voice for both sides. Ara ("the Record") reads every official position and every "case
    for" and "case against" in an identical tone, so neither side sounds favoured.
 • Attributed, not asserted. Contested facts are attributed to their source on screen ("according
    to…", "supporters say…", "critics argue…"). The narrator never states a contested claim as fact.
 • Neutral language. Narration uses "Israel", "Palestinians", "Hamas", "the war in Gaza". Loaded
    labels from either side are not used in the narrator's voice.
 • Neutral design. No national flag colours are used as design colours. Flags appear only where
    they're part of a real photograph.
 • No generated likenesses or symbols. No AI-generated, drawn or altered image of any real
    person, and no AI-generated religious dress, symbols or flags.
 • No graphic content. No images of violence or casualties from any side.

 Disclaimer — on-screen card (after the title in S04) and the top of
 the video description
 This is an independent documentary made for educational and informational purposes. It does
 not take a side in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or paid
 for by any government, political party, advocacy group or community organisation. Statements of
 fact come from parliamentary records, court judgments, government statements, United Nations
 documents and official reports, shown on screen and listed in the description. The positions of
 different sides are summarised and attributed to them, and given equal weight. Casualty figures
 are attributed to their sources. Some scenes are AI-generated reconstructions, and the narration
 voices are AI-generated. No real person has been AI-generated or altered. If you believe anything
 is inaccurate or unfair to any side, contact [your email] and we will review it promptly.

 Rules for this chapter
 • Golden rule: something on screen reacts to the voice every 1–2 seconds. The only exception is S10,
   a quiet moment: slow motion only, no shakes, no comic effects.
 • Balance: S10 shows both 1948 memories at equal size, for equal time, with labels fading in at the
   same moment. S12 shows both population figures in identical design.
 • No AI likeness of real people. Evatt appears only in his real photo (crop, scale, grade and position
   only).
 • AI B-roll style anchor (add to every AI prompt): Cinematic documentary B-roll, 16:9, natural colour,
   gentle film grain, shallow depth of field, slow camera movement, realistic, no text, no logos, no
   recognisable real people, faces not visible.
 • Never generate: real people, religious dress or symbols, flags, weapons, violence, injured people,
   or military insignia.
 • Captions and credits: every real photo has a small caption (place, year) and a credit in the corner.
