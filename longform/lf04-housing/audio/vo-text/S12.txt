The case against the R B A is simple: Sam’s repayments keep going up, and the R B A is the one raising them. But the R B A’s defence is just as simple. If the R B A lets inflation run, everything gets more expensive, including groceries, petrol and rent. And there’s a twist in the R B A’s own history. For years before twenty twenty-two, interest rates were very low, which made borrowing cheap, and helped push house prices up in the first place.
