WHO REALLY RUNS AUSTRALIA? The Hunt for the
Kingmaker

Production Script v2: real footage first, animation as a layer
Runtime: about 13–14 minutes (about 1,950 spoken words). Timestamps are approximate;
set final ones from the voiceover. Narration: unchanged from v1. The clean Grok file
(kingmaker_voiceover_script.md) still matches this script line for line.




HOW THIS SCRIPT IS BUILT
Every shot has two layers.

BASE (real): always real footage or a real photo. This is what gives the film credibility. In
priority order:

1. [PARL] Official Parliament of Australia footage from ParlView (real events, real people,
    free under Parliament’s conditions).

2. [ARCHIVE PHOTO] Real historical photos: National Archives of Australia (NAA),
    Wikimedia Commons, Flickr (free licences only), US presidential libraries (public
    domain).

3. [B-ROLL] Real location and atmosphere footage from Pexels, Pixabay, Coverr, Mixkit
    (free for monetised use).

4. [DOC] Real documents and data pages: AEC, Hansard, PM Transcripts (CC BY 4.0).

OVERLAY (animation): only when the narration explains something (numbers, systems,
timelines, maps). It sits on top of the base layer.


Layering rules for the editor / Claude Code
    Explain mode: when an overlay starts, the base layer blurs (Gaussian about 14 px),
    darkens by about 30% and keeps moving underneath. The overlay animates in on top,
    timed to the words. When the explanation ends, the overlay leaves and the base snaps
    back to sharp.

    Witness mode: when narration describes a real event or person, show the real footage
      or photo full-screen and sharp, with only a small lower-third label.

      ParlView exception (important): Parliament’s conditions say footage must not be
      digitally manipulated. So never blur, recolour, speed-ramp or crop ParlView footage to
      change what it shows. If you need explain mode during a ParlView moment, cut to B-roll
      for the blurred bed instead. Labels go in a bar below or beside the clip, not burned into
      the people.

      Photos never sit still: every still gets a slow push, pan or parallax (2-layer) move.

      Pace: a new base shot every 3–5 seconds. Never more than 6 seconds on one base
      asset.

      Credit tag: every real asset gets a small corner credit, e.g. “Parliament of Australia /
      ParlView”, “NAA”, “Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA 4.0 / [author]”.


Recurring visual identity
Gold crown motif · case board with six SUSPECT cards and red string · navy, parchment and
gold palette (teal only in Chapter 6) · Playfair Display for titles, Inter for data (both free on
Google Fonts).

SOURCING AND LICENSING GUIDE

1. ParlView (free real footage)
Watch, search and download Senate, House and committee recordings, with archive back to
1991, at aph.gov.au → Watch, Read, Listen → ParlView. You need the date to search.
Conditions: fair and accurate reports only, balanced presentation of differing views, no
party-political or commercial advertising, and no digital manipulation. Credit “Parliament of
Australia / ParlView” on screen.
2. Real photos
   National Archives of Australia. Commonwealth copyright lasts 50 years after the year
   made or first published, so government photos from 1975 and earlier are now public
   domain in Australia. Confirm the photo was taken by a government photographer (e.g.
   Australian Information Service), not a newspaper.

   Trove / National Library of Australia. Any photo taken before 1955 is out of copyright
   in Australia. Good for the early polling-place images.

   Wikimedia Commons. Use Public Domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA. Skip NC and ND
   licences on a monetised channel. Note the author and licence for the credit line.

   Flickr. Use the licence filter “Commercial use allowed”. Credit the photographer.

   US presidential libraries. Photos taken by White House photographers are US
   government works and public domain (useful for Murdoch with US presidents).

   Unsplash: only standard free photos. “Unsplash+” images are paid.


3. Free B-roll
Pexels, Pixabay, Coverr, Mixkit. Avoid clips with clear brand logos or readable real campaign
material from other countries.


4. Real documents (CC BY 4.0)
PM Transcripts (pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au) text is licensed CC BY 4.0, except the Coat of
Arms and photos/graphics. Credit: “Source: Licensed from the Commonwealth of Australia
under CC BY 4.0”. AEC web pages and data for the money and results charts.


5. Never use without a licence
TV network news footage (ABC, Nine, Seven, Ten, Sky), newspaper pages or photos (News
Corp, Nine/Fairfax, AAP, Getty), and NFSA footage. If you want the famous 1975 Parliament
steps footage, license it from the NFSA or the broadcaster. YouTube Content ID doesn’t care
about fair dealing arguments.


6. Music and sound
Pixabay Music and YouTube Audio Library. Mood map: investigative pulse (open, Ch. 1),
retro synth (Ch. 2), industrial percussion (Ch. 3), dark strings (Ch. 4), playful plucks (Ch. 5),
modern electronic (Ch. 6–7), solo piano into a warm swell (reveal).
