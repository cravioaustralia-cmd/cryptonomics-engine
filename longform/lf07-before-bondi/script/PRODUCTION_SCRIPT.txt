 1. Editorial stance: we do not take a side
 This documentary does not take a side in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Its question
 is narrower: how has this conflict affected Australia and Australians? It reports what happened,
 shows the evidence on screen as each claim is spoken, and gives every side's position in its
 own terms, at equal weight. It does not tell viewers what to conclude.


 How neutrality is built into every frame
 • Equal treatment. Whenever one community's experience or position is shown, the other's is shown
   at the same size, for the same time, in the same design, with the same music level, joined by the
   amber Seam (motif M3).
 • Neither voice belongs to a side. Atlas and Ara alternate every segment by structure, not by topic.
   Whenever both sides' positions are given in one segment, the same voice reads both, in an identical
   tone.
 • Quotes from both directions. The quote cards include the Australian government, the opposition,
   Israel's prime minister, a pro-Palestinian advocacy group, ASIO and a victim's family, all in the same
   card design.
 • Attributed, not asserted. Contested facts are attributed on screen. The narrators never state a
   contested claim as fact.
 • Neutral language and design. "Israel", "Palestinians", "Hamas", "the war in Gaza". No national
   flag colours as design colours.
 • No generated likenesses or symbols, no graphic content.

 Disclaimer — top of the video description (full version)
 This is an independent documentary made for educational and informational purposes. It does
 not take a side in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or paid
 for by any government, political party, advocacy group or community organisation. Statements of
 fact come from parliamentary records, court judgments, government statements, United Nations
 documents and official reports, shown on screen and listed in the description. Quotations are
 short, attributed, and used for news reporting and commentary. The positions of different sides
 are summarised, attributed, and given equal weight. Casualty figures are attributed to their

 4. Rules (apply to every frame)
 Legal and accuracy
 • Evidence-led. Every factual claim appears on screen with its source and date as it is spoken.
 • "Allegedly" and "according to ASIO" stay attached to the Iran attribution (S05, S09, S10).
 • No causal claim about Bondi. The film never says the Middle East conflict, recognition, protests or
   any group caused the attack. S07 states this to the viewer directly; S41 repeats it with police's stated
   motive and the risk of blaming communities.
 • Bondi: never name or show the gunmen. The surviving accused is before the courts; say only what
   police have stated.
 • Quotes are short, exact, attributed (speaker, date, source) and shown on a quote card as they are
   read. Never edit a quote's words. Check each one against its primary source (section 9).
 • "Summary of public positions" labels every paraphrased case-for / case-against card.
 • Analysis is labelled (Chapter 7 carries an ANALYSIS corner tag).
 • Casualty figures are always attributed, with an as-of date.
 • ParlView excerpts are fair and in context, credited "Parliament of Australia", under Parliament's
   conditions (no party advertising, election material or satire). Get written confirmation before
   publishing.

 People and respect
 • No AI-generated, drawn, animated or altered likeness of any real person. Real people
   appear only in real photos and ParlView footage. Animated people are faceless figure icons.
 • No icons, illustrations or AI images of religious symbols, religious dress, flags or party
   logos. Real symbols appear only where they are in real photos (e.g. menorahs at the Bondi
   memorial).
 • No graphic content, ever. Aftermath, memorials, maps and documents only.
 • Quiet moments (S01, S15, S24, S36, S37, S45): slow, minimal motion; no stamps, shakes or comic
   effects. S38–S41 are near-quiet. No countdown tick sound during quiet moments.
 • Zomi Frankcom's photo only with written family permission; otherwise the name card. Ahmed al
   Ahmed's photo only if a freely licensed real photo exists; otherwise the name card.
 • "Dramatised reconstruction" label on AI01, AI02, AI03 and AI10 whenever they appear.

 Budget and copyright
 • $0. Every item is public domain, Creative Commons, free stock, free under the owner's published
   conditions, generated with your own Grok account, or built by the pipeline.
 • No news-network footage, no agency photos, no screenshots of news websites. Quotes are
   rebuilt as your own quote cards.
 • Items marked PERMISSION / TERMS are used only after written confirmation; each has a free fallback.

 5. The two voices

 • Equal partners. Both voices tell the story, read records and read quotes. Neither is the "main"
   narrator, so after a minute or two viewers expect the handover and listen for it.
 • Strict alternation. Atlas speaks every odd segment, Ara every even one. Segments run 15–40
   seconds, so the switch resets attention without any visual trick.
 • Handover rule. Leave 0.35–0.5 s of air at every handover. Where a segment ends on a question or a
   hook, the next voice answers it, which makes the two sound like they're telling one story together.
 • Never by side. Voices are assigned by position in the script, not by topic. When one segment gives
   two sides' positions, one voice reads both.
 • Reading quotes. The narrator says "quote", takes a short pause, and reads the words plainly,
   without imitating the speaker. The quote card is on screen at the same time.
 • Matching sound. Generate both voices with the same output format and loudness (−14 LUFS
   integrated). If one sounds closer or brighter, EQ it to match before the mix.

 Audition before generating
 Generate S10 and S11 in both candidate female voices next to Atlas. Pick the voice that sounds equally
 neutral reading a record (S10) and naturally paired with Atlas on the handover.
