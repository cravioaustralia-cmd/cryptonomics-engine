So put both sides next to each other, without declaring a winner. Anthony Albanese and Labor hold government, ninety-four seats, control of the budget and Parliament’s agenda, a strong party organisation, and Labor’s seventy-five per cent rule, which makes the leader hard to remove. Their pressures are falling polls, a record-low approval rating, rising interest rates, and a broken tax promise. Pauline Hanson and One Nation hold momentum: first place in Newspoll, a commanding lead in Queensland, a first federal lower-house seat, four lower-house seats in South Australia, four senators, a fundraising campaign that One Nation says raised millions, and growing influence over the Coalition’s choices. Their limits are two House seats, majority disapproval of their leader, weaker support in Victoria and South Australia, and a history of collapsing after past peaks.
