To find out what has actually changed, look beyond the national number. Newspoll’s quarterly breakdown, which surveyed four thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven voters between July and September, shows One Nation’s support varies sharply by state. In Queensland, Pauline Hanson’s home state, One Nation was on thirty-six per cent, eleven points ahead of Labor. In New South Wales, One Nation edged Labor thirty-one to thirty. In Western Australia, Labor led One Nation by a single point. But in Victoria, Labor led twenty-nine to twenty-five. Queensland voters even preferred Hanson to Albanese as prime minister, fifty-two to forty-eight.
