Long-form — Production Script (FINAL)

Can Albanese Stop Pauline Hanson?
Format: long-form, 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps Runtime: about 17½–18 minutes as approved
(2,470 narrated words at the varied paces in Part 3, plus pauses and end screen). This is
above the brief’s 10–15 minute target; the optional trim pack in Part 13 brings it to about 16
minutes without removing anything the brief requires. Mid-roll ad breaks: 3 (after S13, S25
and S33) Voice: Grok TTS, voice Atlas. Delivery direction is in every segment; paste-ready
text is in Part 7. Media budget: $0. Every clip, photo, sound and track comes from a free
source. No news-network or agency footage. Continuity: this is the third film in the series,
after The Pauline Hanson Story and From Council Flat to The Lodge (Albanese). It reuses
their visual language, their two threads, and several of their assets (marked REUSE below).
Research cut-off: 5 October 2026. Re-check polls, interest rates, the Victorian election
and any leadership news on publish day (Part 11).

PART 2: Rules (apply to every frame)

Evidence, balance and law
 1. Every factual claim appears on screen with its source and date as it’s spoken.

 2. The four labels. Every evidence card and key line carries one corner label:

        FACT (white): verified events, official results and records.

        CLAIM (orange outline): something a person or party says, e.g. One Nation’s
        fundraising figures.

        ANALYSIS (blue): an attributed interpretation by a named analyst or outlet.

        SPECULATION (grey, dashed): what could happen. Used only in the ending.

 3. Every poll card shows pollster, fieldwork dates, sample size (where known), and the
    line “One poll is a snapshot, not a verdict.”

 4. No winner is declared. The comparison shows both sides’ strengths and limits.

 5. No motives are invented. Where a motive isn’t documented, the script describes the
    action and attributes any interpretation.

 6. Hanson’s 2003 conviction is always paired with “overturned on appeal”.

 7. ParlView footage in fair, in-context excerpts, credited “Parliament of Australia”, under
    Parliament’s conditions for broadcast material. Written confirmation from ParlView is
    held before publishing.

 8. Not election material. No paid promotion close to an election without advice.

 9. A final review of the cut by an Australian media lawyer.


People and respect
10. No AI-generated, drawn, animated or altered likeness of any real person. People in
    animations are faceless figure icons.

11. The burqa protest appears only as plain ParlView footage with a date lower-third.
    Nothing is animated on top of it, and no icons represent any religious or ethnic group.
12. The Faruqi quote is shown as on-screen text with the bleeped word (“go back to
    Pakistan” is spoken; the expletive is not used).

13. A small “Dramatised reconstruction” label the first time AI B-roll appears.

reading a page. The pace and tone change with what’s being said.

3.2 How the modes are produced in Grok
    Each segment is generated separately with the same Atlas voice settings.

    Pauses are written into the Grok text: [pause] (short) and [long-pause] (longer).
     <slow>…</slow> is used for WEIGHT lines and <soft>…</soft> for quiet lines.

    Speed: every segment lists a target pace. After generating, check the duration. If it’s
    more than about 5% off the target, adjust the clip speed by up to ±6% (pitch-
    preserving) or regenerate. Never speed up a WEIGHT line.

    Retakes: if a segment sounds flat or reads a number wrongly, regenerate it before
    adjusting speed.

    Breathing room: leave 0.3–0.6 seconds of room tone between segments, and longer
   where an edit gap (         ) is listed.


3.3 Emphasis rules
   Numbers that carry the story (94, 30, 27, 2, 36) get a tiny beat before them.

   Contrasts (“power and popularity are not the same thing”) are read slower on the
   second half.

   Questions that end a section are read in WEIGHT mode, then followed by a             1–1.5s
   edit gap with music only.

   Quotes from real people are read neutrally, never in an impression of the speaker.

4.2 The two threads (continuity from the previous films)
   The gold thread belongs to Albanese (from From Council Flat to The Lodge).

   The red thread belongs to Hanson (from The Pauline Hanson Story). In this film it runs
   in One Nation orange-red (#E8552A), so it never clashes with Labor’s red in the
   charts.

   The opening shows both threads starting from opposite ends of the screen.

   Each time the story switches between the two leaders, the camera follows that
   leader’s thread. When they clash (the fundraising war, censures, the polls crossing),
   the two threads cross and knot.

   In the big comparison, the two threads run side by side, one labelled POWER and
   one labelled PRESSURE.

   In the final shot, the threads stretch off toward an unfinished 2028 node, tied to
   nothing yet.


4.3 The scoreboard (the device that holds the story together)
A slim HUD bar at the top of the screen appears whenever numbers are discussed and
updates as the story moves:

ALBANESE · Seats 94/150 · Newspoll 27% · Net approval −27 │ HANSON · Seats 2/150 ·
Senators 4 · Newspoll 30%

   The left half (gold) tracks power; the right half (orange-red) tracks pressure.

   Numbers tick when the narration changes them; the label flashes FACT or CLAIM as
   appropriate.

   It reappears in every chapter, so viewers always know “where the score is”.


4.4 Recurring visual devices
   The poll Polaroid: every poll appears as a Polaroid photo that drops onto the desk with
   a shutter click. The caption reads: pollster, dates, sample, and “A snapshot, not a
   verdict”. Multiple polls stack, showing that they disagree.

   The chamber: a 150-seat House of Representatives hemicycle (Labor red, Coalition
   blue, One Nation orange, Greens green, independents teal, others grey), with a glowing
   “76” majority line.

   The state heat map: a simplified Australia map where states fill with colour intensity as
   polls are read.

   The ballot explainer: an animated green House ballot paper and white Senate ballot
   paper for the mechanics chapter.

   The split-flap year board, stamps and evidence cards carry over from the previous
   films.

4.6 Image animation moves
DROP (photo falls in, shutter click) · STACK (photos pile up) · PAN (slow parallax push) ·
CIRCLE (marker circle) · CALLOUT (label pop) · ZOOM (document dive and highlighter) · PIN
(photo snaps to map or thread) · SPLIT (two photos lock together) · WALL (rapid grid) ·
TORN (paper-rip reveal). Every image is on screen 1.5–3 seconds and always moving.


4.7 AI B-roll
Style anchor: Cinematic documentary B-roll, 16:9, natural colour, gentle film grain, shallow
depth of field, slow camera movement, realistic, no text, no logos, no recognisable real
people, faces not visible. Never generate: real people, party logos, campaign material,
religious dress, or Parliament interiors posing as real events.


4.8 Sound
   Mix: narration −14 LUFS; sound effects about 12 dB under narration; music about 20 dB
   under, ducking under speech.

   Music beds: tense pulse for the opening; measured investigative underscore for Parts
   One, Three and the comparison; momentum build for Part Two; a two-tone “chess” motif
   for Parts Four and Five; light, curious plucks for the mechanics chapter; a reflective,
   unresolved piano for the ending.
     Signature sounds: a thread “zip” when a thread pulls something in; a soft knot “tick”
     when threads cross; a shutter click for each poll Polaroid; a stamp thud for FACT
     stamps.

     Mid-rolls: a clean 0.5-second beat after S13, S25 and S33.
